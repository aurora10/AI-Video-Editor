# Reporting a result

The user judges two things: **what the video looks like**, and **whether your claims are true**.

## Every time you finish a render, say four things

1. **Where it is** — the exact file path, e.g. `studio/out/test-vid-window1.mp4`.
2. **How long it is** — program duration, and the source window it came from.
3. **What you decided** — the cut-plan categories and how many, the captions' style, anything you
   rejected and why.
4. **What you did not do** — the honest gap. "I did not check whether the cut looks jumpy; the
   frame analysis is not built."

Quote the renderer's own lines rather than paraphrasing them.

## Never say these

- "Looks great" / "the captions look good" — you cannot see the video.
- "Everything works" — say which checks ran and what they returned.
- "I applied all the notes" — say which ones, and note that `free` notes are yours to act on.

## Do say

- "Captions: 37 pages, one word at a time, 12/12 sampled frames carry text, 0 pixels over the face."
- "I cut 3 stumbles and kept 320 ms after the claim «Бог очень слабый»."
- "The 6:50–7:48 window has a clean opening line and ends on a rhetorical question that works."
- "This is a 540p preview. The 1080p export is not built yet."

## Reviewing their footage, in their language

If the transcript is Russian, quote the Russian lines you are reasoning about — they recognise their
own sentences instantly and it shows you actually read it.
